\begin{abstract}
RGB--thermal fusion systems can produce visually persuasive outputs even when
clock alignment, geometric registration, or sensor validity does not support
pixel-level combination. We study fusion as selective prediction rather than a
mandatory rendering operation. PRISM-EGT is a dual-encoder method that
conditions a per-pixel thermal contribution on mission task and optional
relative vehicle motion while exposing modality reliability, abstention, and
predictive uncertainty. A non-learned evidence envelope makes thermal
contribution no greater than the product of declared validity, registration,
and timing support for every input and every parameter setting. The method
returns both a human-visible luminance fusion and a reversible named-channel
tensor containing the source modalities and uncertainty outputs. We define a
leakage-aware evaluation over LLVIP, MSRS, and Caltech Aerial RGB-T with
downstream perception, selective-risk, corruption, and latency outcomes. This
manuscript is an implementation-complete protocol draft; quantitative claims
will be added only after the baselines, evaluators, and final model are frozen.
\footnote{A large language model assisted with software scaffolding, design
exploration, and manuscript drafting. No generated statement is treated as an
experimental observation. The human author must independently review and take
responsibility for the method, claims, code, and final results before
submission.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Visible cameras preserve texture and color but can fail under darkness, haze,
or low contrast. Long-wave infrared sensors often preserve target contrast
while losing visible appearance and fine texture. Infrared--visible image
fusion (IVIF) attempts to combine these complementary observations, ranging
from reconstruction objectives to downstream task supervision
\citep{tang2022seafusion,zhao2023cddfuse,liu2023paif}. In practice, the
modalities can also disagree because of parallax, calibration drift, timing,
dead pixels, saturation, or modality-specific degradation.

An unconstrained fusion network may convert those disagreements into a
confident image. This is a particular concern for small uncrewed aircraft,
where image evidence is combined with state estimates and ultimately shown to
an operator or used by perception software. Camera poses support mapping and
multi-view reconstruction, but they do not make an unsupported pixel
correspondence valid \citep{carlone2020vnav}.

We formulate fusion as \emph{selective, evidence-bounded prediction}. The
network may recommend a mission interpretation and spatially varying thermal
contribution, but explicit sensor evidence remains authoritative. Our research
questions are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Does evidence-bounded fusion retain downstream performance under clean
  conditions while lowering selective risk under misregistration, timing, and
  sensor corruptions?
  \item Are predicted abstention and uncertainty calibrated well enough to
  support operator and machine triage?
  \item What is the cost of the hard evidence envelope, task conditioning, and
  relative pose/time context?
\end{enumerate}

The intended contributions are an architectural fusion invariant, a reversible
operator/machine output contract, and a scene-grouped cross-dataset evaluation.
We deliberately do not claim priority or superiority before completing the
registered experiments and broader literature review.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Infrared--visible fusion.}
SeaFusion placed fusion in the loop of semantic perception
\citep{tang2022seafusion}. CDDFuse decomposed correlated and modality-specific
representations \citep{zhao2023cddfuse}, while PAIF considered task performance
under adversarial perturbation \citep{liu2023paif}. More recent work studies
semantic guidance, controllable fusion, and task-adaptive fusion
\citep{zhang2024semantic,yang2026customized,guo2026confusion}. These results
make task conditioning alone an insufficient novelty claim.

\paragraph{Registration and reliability.}
SFRF jointly estimates registration uncertainty and fuses spatial/frequency
evidence \citep{li2026sfrf}. C2RF jointly learns cross-modal registration and
fusion through commonality mining and contrastive learning
\citep{tang2025c2rf}. Reliability-aware routing has also been proposed
for misaligned RGB--infrared UAV detection \citep{hou2026leryolo}. PRISM-EGT
does not claim to replace those registration systems. It accepts an external
registration-support map and constrains learned contribution by that evidence,
while separately learning to abstain from residual conflicts.

\paragraph{Selective prediction and calibration.}
Selective classification evaluates a predictor jointly with its decision to
abstain \citep{geifman2017selective}. Modern neural networks can remain
miscalibrated even when their accuracy is high \citep{guo2017calibration}.
We transfer risk--coverage and calibration evaluation to dense multimodal
fusion rather than treating uncertainty as a purely visual auxiliary output.

\section{Method}

Let $V\in[0,1]^{3\times H\times W}$ be registered visible RGB, $T\in
[0,1]^{H\times W}$ normalized thermal evidence, and $E=(E_v,E_r,E_t)$ be
validity, registration, and timing support maps. The non-learned envelope is

\begin{equation}
  S(p)=E_v(p)E_r(p)E_t(p), \qquad S(p)\in[0,1].
\end{equation}

Separate residual convolutional encoders produce $F_v$ and $F_t$. The selector
uses $[F_v,F_t,|F_v-F_t|,F_v\odot F_t,E]$, enabling it to represent agreement,
disagreement, and declared support. A task embedding for Navigate, Search, or
Terrain and an optional normalized relative pose/time vector apply feature-wise
affine conditioning. The latter contains translation, rotation, inter-frame
time, and timing uncertainty; raw absolute latitude/longitude is excluded to
reduce geographic shortcut learning.

The network predicts modality logits, a thermal gate $g_T$, learned abstention
$a_L$, and learned uncertainty $u_L$. With thermal reliability $r_T$ from a
two-way softmax, the outputs are

\begin{align}
  a(p) &= \max(a_L(p),1-S(p)),\\
  \alpha_T(p) &= r_T(p)g_T(p)S(p)(1-a(p)),\\
  Y_F(p) &= (1-\alpha_T(p))Y_V(p)+\alpha_T(p)T(p),\\
  u(p) &= \max(u_L(p),1-S(p)(1-a(p))).
\end{align}

Because $r_T,g_T,S,$ and $(1-a)$ lie in $[0,1]$,

\begin{equation}
  0\leq\alpha_T(p)\leq S(p)
\end{equation}

for all inputs and parameter values. Therefore $S(p)=0$ forces zero thermal
contribution, complete abstention, and maximum reported uncertainty. The fused
luminance is a convex mixture of measured sources; visible chroma is retained
only for the operator rendering.

The machine projection preserves $V$, $T$, $E$, registration support, and the
actual thermal contribution, then appends visible/thermal reliability,
abstention, predictive uncertainty, and fused luminance. The human rendering
is not the sole machine input. Every checkpoint carries its architecture,
training provenance, validation scope, metrics, and SHA-256 identity.

\subsection{Training objective}

There is no unique fused-image ground truth. The bootstrap objective therefore
combines maximum-source gradient retention, task-weighted convex intensity
targets, corruption-supervised abstention, Brier-style uncertainty calibration,
task classification, and a small contribution-entropy term. Synthetic training
corruptions include declared and hidden translations, dropped thermal regions,
and thermal noise. These proxy losses optimize the selector but cannot establish
scientific superiority; primary evidence comes from frozen downstream tasks and
held-out selective-risk tests.

\section{Experimental protocol}

We use LLVIP for paired low-light pedestrian scenes
\citep{jia2021llvip}, MSRS/MFNet for road semantics
\citep{ha2017mfnet,tang2022seafusion}, and Caltech Aerial RGB-T for natural
aerial terrain \citep{lee2024caltech}. LLVIP capture-sequence prefixes and
Caltech flight/scene groups never cross partitions. The complete MSRS publisher
test split remains final-test-only. Table~\ref{tab:counts} reports the executable
protocol counts.

Baselines will include RGB-only, thermal-only, average and maximum luminance,
deterministic OpenPRISM, and frozen implementations representing
reconstruction, perception-aware, and registration-aware learned fusion. The
predefined ablations remove task conditioning, abstention, calibration loss,
hidden-corruption training, the hard envelope, and relative pose/time context.

LLVIP detection uses one unchanged Faster R-CNN ResNet-50-FPN-v2 COCO probe
for every view. The evaluator records the detector-weight checksum and reports
AP@[.50:.95], AP50, and log-average miss rate. Human-facing color output and
machine-facing PRISM-EGT luminance are reported separately. This visible-domain
probe is not treated as an unbiased thermal detector or as the sole measure of
downstream utility.

Primary outcomes are LLVIP person-detection AP and miss rate, MSRS and Caltech
semantic mIoU, risk--coverage area, Brier score, expected calibration error,
abstention AUROC, and the maximum support-bound violation. We additionally
report scene-group bootstrap confidence intervals, latency, memory, parameters,
fusion metrics, and failure cases. No-reference visual metrics are secondary.

\section{Limitations}

Publisher rectification is not equivalent to measured live registration.
Per-image thermal normalization is not radiometric temperature. Bootstrap task
labels may permit dataset shortcuts, so reliable automatic mission selection
requires mission-context supervision or downstream differentiable objectives.
Synthetic corruptions cannot substitute for hardware clock, calibration, and
flight testing. The method does not provide navigation state estimation and
must not be placed in the flight-control safety loop without an independent
assurance process.

\subsubsection*{Broader impact statement}

RGB--thermal perception can support search and rescue, environmental mapping,
inspection, and safer low-light operation. It can also increase surveillance
capability, enable privacy-invasive tracking, or produce dangerous confidence
when deployed outside validation conditions. OpenPRISM mitigates—but cannot
eliminate—these risks through abstention, source preservation, visible
uncertainty, explicit provenance, and a prohibition on learned overrides of
sensor eligibility. Operators remain responsible for applicable aviation,
privacy, and data-protection requirements.
